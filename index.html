Kinematic vs Overconstrained: the 6-strut rover release mount

Drag the model to orbit it. Click a strut to delete it, click empty space near a ring to add one back. The physics is real — the right panel solves an actual 6×6 stiffness system, not a canned animation.
This is the mount problem behind the Starship↔rover 6-DOF release interface: JAXA wants more compliance margin, SpaceX wants fewer/simpler attach points. The instinct is "add more struts, it'll be stiffer and safer." It isn't. A rigid body has exactly 6 degrees of freedom. Six struts, each pulling along its own axis, can pin down exactly those 6 DOF with zero redundancy — a kinematic (exactly-constrained) mount. A 7th or 8th strut doesn't add stiffness once the body is already fully constrained; it adds a redundant load path that fights the other struts every time there's a thermal gradient or a tenth-of-a-millimeter manufacturing mismatch. Try the tolerance slider below and watch the preload appear out of nowhere on the 8-strut mount while the 6-strut mount stays at zero.
