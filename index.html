Kinematic vs Overconstrained: 6 rigid joints on a rover mount

Drag the model to orbit it. Click a joint to delete it, click again to add it back. Each of the 6 real connections reacts up to 6 components (3 forces + 3 moments) — not 1 axis like a pin-ended rod. The right panel solves an actual 6×6 stiffness system for whatever combination you build, live.
Corrected model: the Starship↔rover release interface has 6 locations, and each one is a rigid, moment-carrying joint (a clamped separation-nut cup, not a pin-ended strut). A single fully-rigid joint already reacts all 6 degrees of freedom of the rover — so six of them is not "just enough," it's 30 redundant reaction components by construction (36 provided vs. 6 needed). That overconstraint is deliberate: you want 6 load paths for strength and fault tolerance, not because kinematics requires it. The cost is that any thermal growth or machining mismatch at one joint has nowhere geometrically consistent to go — it shows up as real preload, unless somewhere in the stack there's dedicated flexibility to absorb it. Switch the joint type below (pin → ball → rigid) to see the redundancy count explode, then use the lander-side cup compliance slider to see how local flexibility — not fewer joints — is what actually relieves the self-stress. That's the real design lever you used: compliance on the lander side, not in the release joint.

Model: N joints evenly spaced on a rover ring, each directly above a matching lander point. Pin = 1 reaction row (axial, vertical). Ball = 3 rows (vertical + local radial + local tangential force). Rigid = ball's 3 force rows plus 3 pure-moment rows about the same 3 local axes — a full 6×6 reaction block per joint, i.e. an RBE2-style rigid connection rather than a CROD pin. Global stiffness K = Σ kᵢ·rowᵢ⊗rowᵢ solved directly (Gaussian elimination, no canned answers) for an applied lunar-gravity wrench and for an imposed length-error vector at the selected joint/axis. Translational stiffness ≈ steel ⌀10mm bolt/rod; rotational stiffness is a representative bolted-flange value (both order-of-magnitude, not a specific hardware spec). Rover assumed 1200 kg, lunar g = 1.62 m/s².
